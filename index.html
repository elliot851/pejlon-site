The cost

Half an hour to compare five ad accounts. Every time you want to know where you stand.

None of the work below produces anything. It is the tax you pay to find out what already happened, and you pay it again tomorrow. Here is where it goes.

Account switching

Every account in its own tab

You click back and forth, hold the figures in your head, and hope you picked the same date range everywhere. One tab on last 7 days and one on last 14 is a decision made on the wrong number.

The spreadsheet

Export, paste, rebuild

The columns you actually read are not the columns you get. So it goes into a sheet, and by the afternoon the sheet is already stale.

The column view

Set up again, every time

Ads Manager forgets what you care about. Hook rate and hold rate are three menus deep, and they are the two you check first.

Upload

One ad at a time

Same twelve clicks per ad. Thirty ads is thirty times twelve clicks, and each one is a chance to pick the wrong page or paste the wrong link.

The winner

Rebuilt from scratch elsewhere

A creative that works in one account gets recreated in the next, and starts again from zero likes and zero comments. The social proof you earned does not come with it.

Day by day

Checked row by row, by hand

To find out whether a number is steady you open each row and read the days one at a time. Which means in practice nobody does it.

One table

Every account, every level, every column. In one place.

Campaigns, ad sets and ads side by side, in the columns an advertiser actually reads, in the order they read them. Sort on anything. Filter on anything. What you do in the table applies everywhere at once.

Reading order

The columns are already right

Spend, ROAS, CPC, outbound CTR, purchases, CPA, ATC, hook rate, hold rate, CPM. Nothing to configure, nothing three menus deep.

Sort and filter

Find the three rows that matter

Sort on any column, filter by status, name or market, and switch between campaign, ad set and ad level without losing your place.

In place

Change it where you see it

Flip a campaign on or off with the switch, click a budget to change it. Turning something off always asks first, so nothing goes dark by accident.

Day by day

A 3× ROAS means nothing until you know what the days look like.

A seven day average hides everything that matters. One strong day can carry a week, and scaling on that number is how a good account turns bad in three days. Pejlon keeps the days behind every row and says so when they don't hold together.

Behind every row

Yesterday, and the day before

Today against yesterday against the day before, on the metrics you actually decide on: ROAS, CPC, CTR, purchases, CPA. No export, no second tab.

The brake

It says no as well as yes

When a row looks strong over the period but is uneven across the days, the suggestion is hold, not raise. It is the single most common way a scale up goes wrong.

The analysis

It reads every row for you and tells you what it would do.

You should not have to go through thirty rows to find the three worth touching. The analysis works through the whole account, weighs each row against your targets, and leaves one suggestion per row: raise, cut or hold. Every suggestion comes with the reason written out.

With an amount

Not "this looks good"

How much, and why now, measured against your ROAS target and the ceiling on what an order is allowed to cost.

Inherit

Copy from an ad that works

Page, button, link and copy come from an existing ad. You change what you want to change; the rest is already right.

Before, not after

You see the ad first

Control

Nothing spends money without you saying so.

A tool that sits on your ad account has to be careful by default. Every choice here leans the same way: the quiet option is the safe one, and the expensive one takes a deliberate yes.

Paused by default

New ads don't start themselves

Ads are created paused unless you explicitly choose otherwise, and the button says which it is going to be before you press it.

Confirm

Turning things off asks first

Pausing a campaign, an ad set or an ad takes a confirmation, so nothing goes dark from a stray click in a long table.

Meta's defaults off

Advantage+ enhancements stay off

Meta switches its own creative changes on unless told not to. Pejlon says no on your behalf, per feature, unless you tick it.

Move the winner

Clone with the proof intact

Copy an ad that works to other accounts and keep the likes and comments, instead of starting again from zero social proof.

Media library

Reuse what you already uploaded

Everything already in the ad account is one click away, so the same creative does not get uploaded five times under five different IDs.

The log

Every change recorded

What changed, when, and who did it. When a number moves next week you can find out what happened instead of guessing.

And what it isn't: Pejlon is not an attribution tool. The figures are Meta's own. If you want to model what Meta gets wrong, there are tools that do that better than we ever will, and you should use one. We shorten the work between the numbers and the decision.
